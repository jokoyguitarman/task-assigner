What changed here

Compared with the screen your staff use today.

  • Grouped by area, not by status. Someone working the kitchen reads one short list.
  • Sorted by closest deadline. Today the order is whatever the database returned.
  • Each row shows time remaining, which is only possible now that tasks have a time at all.
  • Late work turns red on this device immediately — this is the branch's own nudge, before you are ever told.
  • Unclaimed work says so, and tapping it lets whoever is doing it put their name on it.
Deliberately absent: no create-task button, no priority picker, no subtasks, no filters to configure. They are running a restaurant, not project management software.

What gets sent, and what does not

The rule is that a notification must be worth the interruption.

  • Sent: work still outstanding 30 minutes past its deadline.
  • Sent once a day, optional: a morning summary of how last night closed.
  • Not sent: anything finished inside the grace window.
  • Not sent: ordinary completions. You do not need to know the bins went out, only that they did not.
  • Not sent to staff. They have no accounts and no app. The branch phone showing red is their nudge.
Honest limitation: this arrives reliably on Android and desktop. On iPhone it only works if you add the app to your home screen first, which is an Apple restriction rather than a choice. If that matters, the alternative is paying per message for WhatsApp or Viber, which we can add later without changing anything else.

Before and after

What is genuinely different once this ships.

Today

  • Tasks have no time, so nothing is late until midnight.
  • "22 minutes late" cannot be expressed or measured.
  • Staff see one long list ordered by whatever came back from the database.
  • Your dashboard shows totals for everything and never says which branch has a problem.
  • You find out something was missed by opening the app and reading.
  • A closing task and an opening task are indistinguishable to the system.

After

  • Every task is due at a real hour, set once per shift per branch.
  • On time, late and missed become numbers you can see per person and per weekday.
  • Staff see one short checklist per area, closest deadline first.
  • Your dashboard leads with the branch that needs you and hides the ones that do not.
  • Your phone tells you, 30 minutes after a deadline is blown.
  • A 24-hour branch, or one with no counter, gets exactly the work that applies to it.
What this still is not. No proof that someone physically stood at the freezer, and no temperature readings — you set both aside for now. Both stay easy to add later, and neither blocks anything above.
